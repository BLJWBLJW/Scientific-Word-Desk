\section*{Explicit Form of the Second Pfaffian Compound $\mathcal{P}_2(A)$ for a $4 \times 4$ Skew-Symmetric Matrix $A$}

Consider a general $4 \times 4$ skew-symmetric matrix:
\[
A = \begin{pmatrix}
0 & a_{12} & a_{13} & a_{14} \\
- a_{12} & 0 & a_{23} & a_{24} \\
- a_{13} & - a_{23} & 0 & a_{34} \\
- a_{14} & - a_{24} & - a_{34} & 0 \\
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The \textbf{second Pfaffian compound matrix} $\mathcal{P}_2(A)$ is constructed from the Pfaffians of all $2 \times 2$ principal submatrices of $A$.

\subsection*{Step 1: Identify the $2 \times 2$ Principal Submatrices}
There are $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ principal submatrices. Their Pfaffians are:
\begin{align*}
\text{Pf}(A_{\{1,2\}}) &= a_{12}, \\
\text{Pf}(A_{\{1,3\}}) &= a_{13}, \\
\text{Pf}(A_{\{1,4\}}) &= a_{14}, \\
\text{Pf}(A_{\{2,3\}}) &= a_{23}, \\
\text{Pf}(A_{\{2,4\}}) &= a_{24}, \\
\text{Pf}(A_{\{3,4\}}) &= a_{34}.
\end{align*}

\subsection*{Step 2: Construct $\mathcal{P}_2(A)$ as a Skew-Symmetric Matrix}
The compound matrix $\mathcal{P}_2(A)$ is a $6 \times 6$ skew-symmetric matrix. Its entries are determined by:
\[
(\mathcal{P}_2(A))_{I,J} = \text{Pf}(A_{I \cup J}) \cdot \text{sgn}(I, J),
\]
where $\text{sgn}(I,J)$ is the permutation sign for merging $I$ and $J$.

\subsection*{Step 3: Explicit Form of $\mathcal{P}_2(A)$}
Ordering rows/columns lexicographically as $\{1,2\}, \{1,3\}, \{1,4\}, \{2,3\}, \{2,4\}, \{3,4\}$, we obtain:
\[
\mathcal{P}_2(A) = \begin{pmatrix}
0 & a_{23} & -a_{24} & -a_{13} & a_{14} & 0 \\
- a_{23} & 0 & a_{34} & a_{12} & 0 & -a_{14} \\
a_{24} & -a_{34} & 0 & 0 & -a_{12} & a_{13} \\
a_{13} & -a_{12} & 0 & 0 & a_{34} & -a_{24} \\
- a_{14} & 0 & a_{12} & -a_{34} & 0 & a_{23} \\
0 & a_{14} & -a_{13} & a_{24} & -a_{23} & 0 \\
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\subsection*{Verification}
The Pfaffian identity holds:
\[
\text{Pf}(\mathcal{P}_2(A)) = \text{Pf}(A)^{\binom{3}{1}} = \text{Pf}(A)^3.
\]

\subsection*{Remarks}
\begin{itemize}
    \item For larger matrices, $\mathcal{P}_{2k}(A)$ follows analogous but more complex constructions.
    \item Applications include fermionic systems in physics and algebraic combinatorics.
\end{itemize}
