\section*{Minimal Left Ideals and Projectors}

A \textbf{minimal left ideal} in a C\*-algebra \( \mathcal{A} \) is generated by a \textbf{minimal projector} \( q \), which is a projection satisfying \( q \mathcal{A} q \cong \mathbb{C} \). The minimal left ideal is then:
\[
\mathcal{I} = \mathcal{A} q = \{ a q \mid a \in \mathcal{A} \}.
\]
The key question is whether \( q \) is a \textbf{1-dimensional projector}.

\section*{When Are Minimal Projectors 1-Dimensional?}

A projector \( q \) is \textbf{1-dimensional} if it projects onto a 1-dimensional subspace. This is true in certain C\*-algebras but not in others:

\subsection*{(a) Finite-Dimensional Algebras (e.g., Matrix Algebras)}

In the C\*-algebra \( \mathcal{A} = M_n(\mathbb{C}) \) (the algebra of \( n \times n \) complex matrices):
\begin{itemize}
    \item A minimal projector \( q \) is a rank-1 matrix (e.g., \( q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \) for \( n = 2 \)).
    \item Such a projector is \textbf{1-dimensional} because it projects onto a 1-dimensional subspace of \( \mathbb{C}^n \).
    \item All minimal left ideals in \( M_n(\mathbb{C}) \) are generated by 1-dimensional projectors.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{(b) Infinite-Dimensional Algebras (e.g., \( B(\mathcal{H}) \))}

In the C\*-algebra \( \mathcal{A} = B(\mathcal{H}) \) (the algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space \( \mathcal{H} \)):
\begin{itemize}
    \item A minimal projector \( q \) corresponds to a projection onto a 1-dimensional subspace of \( \mathcal{H} \).
    \item However, \( B(\mathcal{H}) \) also contains minimal projectors that are \textbf{not 1-dimensional} if \( \mathcal{H} \) is infinite-dimensional. For example, if \( \mathcal{H} \) is separable, there exist minimal projectors that project onto infinite-dimensional subspaces (e.g., projections onto subspaces isomorphic to \( \ell^2 \)).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{(c) General C\*-Algebras}

In a general C\*-algebra:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A minimal projector \( q \) satisfies \( q \mathcal{A} q \cong \mathbb{C} \), but \( q \) itself may not correspond to a 1-dimensional subspace unless the algebra has a specific structure (e.g., it is a finite-dimensional or type I factor).
\end{itemize}

\section*{When Are Minimal Left Ideals Generated by 1-Dimensional Projectors?}

Minimal left ideals are generated by 1-dimensional projectors \textbf{if and only if} the C\*-algebra has the property that all its minimal projectors are 1-dimensional. This is true in:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Finite-dimensional C\*-algebras} (e.g., matrix algebras \( M_n(\mathbb{C}) \)),
    \item \textbf{Type I factors} (e.g., \( B(\mathcal{H}) \) for finite-dimensional \( \mathcal{H} \)).
\end{itemize}

However, in \textbf{infinite-dimensional C\*-algebras} or more general settings, minimal projectors need not be 1-dimensional, and the corresponding minimal left ideals are not generated by 1-dimensional projectors.

\section*{Example: Matrix Algebra \( M_2(\mathbb{C}) \)}

In \( \mathcal{A} = M_2(\mathbb{C}) \):
\begin{itemize}
    \item A minimal projector is of the form \( q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \), which is 1-dimensional.
    \item The minimal left ideal generated by \( q \) is:
    \[
    \mathcal{I} = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a & 0 \\ c & 0 \end{pmatrix} \mid a, b, c, d \in \mathbb{C} \right\}.
    \]
    This ideal is generated by a 1-dimensional projector.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Example: Infinite-Dimensional \( B(\mathcal{H}) \)}

In \( \mathcal{A} = B(\mathcal{H}) \) for an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space \( \mathcal{H} \):
\begin{itemize}
    \item A minimal projector \( q \) may correspond to a projection onto an infinite-dimensional subspace (e.g., \( q \) could be a projection onto \( \ell^2 \)).
    \item The minimal left ideal \( \mathcal{I} = \mathcal{A} q \) is generated by \( q \), but \( q \) is not 1-dimensional.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
    \item In \textbf{finite-dimensional C\*-algebras} (e.g., \( M_n(\mathbb{C}) \)), all minimal left ideals are generated by \textbf{1-dimensional projectors}.
    \item In \textbf{infinite-dimensional C\*-algebras} (e.g., \( B(\mathcal{H}) \)), minimal left ideals may be generated by projectors that are \textbf{not 1-dimensional}.
    \item In general, whether minimal left ideals are generated by 1-dimensional projectors depends on the structure of the C\*-algebra and the nature of its minimal projectors.
\end{itemize}
